# odin age-structured SEIR with a delay, in Starsim

The translation of [examples/odin/seir_array](../../../examples/odin/seir_array), odin's wave 3 example. The model is `model.py`; `compare.py` checks it against the original's reference results.

Measured on 14 October 2026 with Starsim 4.0.0 (the `v4` branch, after commit `1f02f769`, with the fixes to findings 4 to 6) on a 20-core x86-64 Linux machine.

## What was declared and what was not

Everything is declared: seven transitions and one stratification, whose aging between groups is generated. The stable age distribution is worked out by hand in two lines of NumPy, as the original works it out in two lines of odin (`den[1]` and `den[2:N_age]`). One transition, `history`, is a workaround (finding 1).

| Original | Starsim |
|---|---|
| `dim(S) <- N_age` and so on, for 9 arrays | `seir.stratify(age=[0, 1, 5, 15, 30], split=split)` |
| `age_width`, `age_rate`, and the aging terms in all eight `deriv()` equations | Generated by the age stratification, at one over each width |
| `new_inf[] <- beta * S[i] * I_tot / N` with `I_tot <- sum(I)` | `S >> E @ ss.perday(1) * I` |
| `lag_inf[] <- delay(new_inf[i] * surv, lat_hum)` | `E >> I @ ss.erlang(k=200, mean=ss.days(14))`, with deaths from `E` doing the work of `surv` |
| `Births <- b * N` into `S[1]`, and `- b * X[i]` in every equation | `ss.birth('-> S', ...)` and `ss.death(...)` |
| `den[]` | `split`, worked out the same way |
| `delay()`'s value before day 14 | `history`, an inflow to `I` for 14 days (finding 1) |

## Findings

1. **The history of a delay (blocks agreement; worked around).** For the first 14 days, odin's `delay(new_inf * surv, 14)` looks back to before day 0 and takes the value at day 0, so about one person a day reaches `I` as if infections had been going on before the start. A model whose latent state starts empty cannot do this, and with a reproduction number near 30, the 14 extra cases bring the epidemic forward by 15 days (without them `I` peaks on day 140, and differs by up to 65% of its peak). The workaround is a transition that adds them: `history = ss.null >> I @ ss.timeseries({0: ss.perday(surv), 14: 0})`. It adds 14 people that the original takes from `E` (which runs 14 short for the rest of the run), a difference of one part in a million. The spec has no way to start a state with a dwell time partway through: `init=dict(E=14)` puts everyone in the first of the 200 stages, so they all leave about day 14, not one a day over the first 14 days. Suggested change: let `init` for a state with a dwell time say that the people are spread over it as if they had arrived at a constant rate (`ss.State(init=14, spread=True)`, or `init=dict(E=ss.arrived(rate=1))`), which as agents is a remaining time drawn uniformly, and as compartments is the same number in each stage.

2. **A fixed delay is many stages, which are slow and still approximate (awkward and slow).** Spec 03 has no fixed dwell time in a compartmental run: any distribution other than an Erlang is matched by Erlang stages, and `ss.constant(ss.days(14))` becomes 50 (`erlang_stages()` caps it there, which the spec does not mention), with a note. The error goes as one over the number of stages and the time as its square, since each stage turns over faster and the RK4 integrator takes more substeps:

    | Stages | Largest difference in `I` | One run |
    |---|---|---|
    | 50 (also `ss.constant`) | 6.3% | 0.2 s |
    | 100 | 3.2% | 0.4 s |
    | 200 (`model.py`) | 1.6% | 1.6 s |
    | 400 | 0.8% | 6 s |

    With `integrator='LSODA'` (or `'BDF'`) a run of 200 stages did not finish in 2 minutes. The original solves the delay exactly in 2 ms. Spec 09 already realizes an outcome's `delay=` in a deterministic compartmental run as "a convolution of the flow with the delay distribution", and the solvers keep a buffer of pending outcomes for it. Suggested change: realize a constant dwell time (and, by convolution, any distribution with a short enough support) in the compartmental solvers in the same way, as a conveyor: a buffer of the people who entered in each recent step, each thinned by the state's other outflows (deaths, aging) and released when its time is up. That is one compartment per step of the delay, not stiff, and exact at the step; and it gives finding 1 a natural form, since the buffer can be filled at the start.

3. **The original's latent do not age (disagreement by age, not a Starsim issue).** In the original, `E` is aged like every state, but `lag_inf[i]` takes from `E[i]` everyone infected in group `i` 14 days earlier, including the 1 in 26 of the youngest who have aged out since. So `E[1]` falls short and `I[1]` gets more than it should; summed over ages nothing is lost. Starsim's stages age with everyone else, so infants infected at 360 days old become infectious as 1–5 year olds. This was checked with a separate Python reimplementation of the original's equations: as written, it matches the reference to within 0.2% in every group; with the latent aging, it differs from the reference in the youngest group by the same 5.0% (`I`) and 8.6% (`R`) as Starsim. Starsim's is the consistent model, so no change is suggested; but a conveyor (finding 2) should age what is in it, as here, not release people by the group they entered.

4. **The first age group was labeled `0-0` (awkward; fixed).** `stratify(age=[0, 1, 5, 15, 30])` labeled its groups `0-0, 1-4, 5-14, 15-29, 30+` (the last whole year in each), so `select(age='0-1')` raised `"0-1" is not a level of "age"`, and `compare.py` had to select by position. Fixed in Starsim: an age group one year wide is now labeled by its single year, so the groups are `0, 1-4, 5-14, 15-29, 30+` and `compare.py` selects by label (`select(age='0')`).

5. **`describe()` misdescribed an inflow and leaves out aging (awkward; half fixed).** An inflow with a time-varying rate was printed as `history  -> I  dwell ~ exponential(rate = timeseries(...))`, which reads as a dwell time of a state that has none. Fixed in Starsim: `history` and `births` are now both printed as `inflow, in total per day`, with their `rate =`. Still open: the aging transfers generated by the age stratification are not listed, though spec 05 says `describe()` lists them, so nothing in the output says that people age, or at what rates. Suggested change: list the generated aging (`aging  0 -> 1-4  rate = 1 per year`, and so on).

6. **Assigning a distribution to a transition after it was made broke the build (bug; fixed).** Changing the dwell time of a disease after it was made failed when the sim was built:

    ```python
    sir = ss.Disease(infection=ss.transmission('S -> I', beta=0.3), recovery=ss.progression('I -> R', dur=ss.days(10)), init=dict(I=1))
    sir.recovery.dur = ss.erlang(k=3, mean=ss.days(10))
    ss.Sim(sir, total_pop=1000, unit='day', stop=10).run() # Was: AttributeError: 'gamma' object has no attribute 'leaves'
    ```

    Fixed in Starsim: assigning `dur` now sets the value of the transition's parameter, and the example above runs as if the disease had been made with that dwell time. `compare.py` now makes its variants this way, not from the text of `model.py`. One thing to know: `ss.Sim(seir)` keeps its own copy of the disease, so assigning `seir.onset.dur` after the sim is made changes nothing in that sim, silently; `compare.py` changes the sim's copy (`sim.inputs['modules'][0].onset.dur`). It would be clearer if the sim either used the disease it was given or said that it had copied it.
