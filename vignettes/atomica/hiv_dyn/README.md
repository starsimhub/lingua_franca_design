# Atomica dynamic HIV demo, in Starsim

The translation of [examples/atomica/hiv_dyn](../../../examples/atomica/hiv_dyn), Atomica's model for wave 3. The model is `model.py`; `compare.py` checks it against the original's reference results.

## Comparison

| Measure | Original | Starsim | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lines of model code | 217 | 67 | The original is counted as rows of its three spreadsheets, plus 29 lines of Python; in Starsim, 12 lines are over 100 characters, so count twice |
| One run | 0.0054 s | 0.18 s | With or without the programs (0.25 s before the fixes below). The original does not count reading the spreadsheets (about 0.2 s); in Starsim most of the time is building the sim, and the step is in Python, not the kernels, because of a function of the sim (finding 2) |
| Results | | | **Identical,** to rounding error (a largest difference of 2 in 10<sup>14</sup>), in every compartment and every level of the cascade of both sexes, in all four runs: without programs, and with the default, doubled, and zero budgets |

Measured on 14 October 2026 with Starsim 4.0.0 (the `v4` branch) on a 20-core x86-64 Linux machine. The first version (13 October) needed the workarounds for findings 1, 3, 5, and 6; this one, after the fixes, does not.

## What was declared and what was not

The transitions (written as Atomica's transition matrix, one per parameter), the parameters, the formulas that turn annual numbers into rates, the births by sex, the cascade by sex, the programs' coverage and effects, and the scenarios are all declared, as expressions of counts and parameters. What is left as a workaround is marked in `model.py`:

- `everyone()`, a function of the sim that counts the eligible in both sexes (finding 2)
- The initial state, worked out from the databook's cascade with `np.diff`, where Atomica solves for it (as in the [SIR demo](../sir))
- `ss.par(...)` around three `ss.by` values, and the expression written before the `ss.by` in `outcome`, because of finding 9

The `leave()` helper (finding 1) and the births given as a total (finding 3) are gone.

## Findings

1. **Fixed: a step that moves more than everyone created people** (blocked agreement without a workaround). Atomica turns each parameter into a fraction moved in a step, and if the fractions leaving a compartment add up to more than 1 it scales them down together. Here that happens every step, since the time to viral suppression is 0.2 years and the step is a year. Starsim's Euler step moves `rate*y*dt` along each flow and then sets negative compartments to 0, so people are created: written as plain transitions with `integrator='euler', substeps=1`, there are 43 million virally suppressed females out of 28.5 million in 2035. With the default integrator (RK4 with substeps), the model is a smooth ODE and disagrees with Atomica's difference equation: 85,000 treated females in 2035, against 445,000. No `W102 LargeStep` note is given. The smallest repro is `ss.Sim(ss.Disease(rec=S >> I @ 5, init=dict(S=100)), total_pop=100, start=2000, stop=2002, integrator='euler', substeps=1)`, which gives 500 in `I` after one step. The workaround is one branching transition per compartment, with a rate of `np.minimum(total, 1)` and each way out a share `rate/total` of it, which is Atomica's rule and agrees exactly. Suggested change: the Euler step (in Python and in the kernel) should scale a compartment's outflows down together when they add up to more than it holds, and say so once with `W102`; Atomica's scheme is then just `integrator='euler', substeps=1`. **Fixed** in Starsim: with `integrator='euler'` and `substeps` given, a compartment's outflows that would take more than everyone are scaled down together, in the kernels and in Python, and `W102` says so for `supp`. The model is now plain transitions, one for each parameter of Atomica's transition matrix, and agrees exactly.
2. **A count is either within one's own group or over everyone, not both** (awkward; costs speed). Atomica's populations each have their own rates, made from their own compartment sizes (`num_acq/max(sus, num_acq)`), but a program's coverage is the number it reaches over everyone eligible in both populations. In Starsim, `.n` in a rate is the total over a dimension that is not a place, and the count in the same place for a dimension that is (the spec, in [03](../../../design/03-states-and-transitions.md), only has the total). So sex is stratified as a place (`kind='space'`, with no mobility, which changes nothing else here), and the totals are a function of the sim, `sum(sim.diseases.hiv[name].n ...)`. That function keeps the run out of the kernels and stops `sim.restart()`. Suggested change: say which in the expression, with `sus.n` the total everywhere (as the spec says) and `sus.n.local`, or `sus.n.within('sex')`, the count in one's own group; then nothing here needs a function, or a place. **Still open.** The same `.n` with sex as an ordinary dimension (`stratify(sex=...)` with no `kind`) is still the total over both sexes (a rate of `0.01*S.n/100` with 100 and 300 people moves 4 and 12, as for 400), so the per-sex rates cannot be written that way, and the place and the function are still needed.
3. **Fixed: an inflow given by group was shared out again** (a bug; would have blocked agreement). `ss.null >> sus @ ss.by(sex=[100, 10])` in a model stratified by sex with `split=[1000, 500]` adds 66.7 females and 3.3 males a step, not 100 and 10: the value for each group is multiplied by the group's share of the split, as an inflow at one fixed rate is. Here the births are the same share of each sex, so the total (564,691) gives the right numbers, but in general this cannot be written. Suggested change: in `Compartmental.inflow_split()`, an inflow whose rate varies by group in the dimensions it is split over arrives as given in each group, as `inflow_local` already does for one that uses a count in a place. **Fixed**: an inflow whose rate is an `ss.by` now arrives at each level as given, and the births are written by sex, as in the databook.
4. **Programs and budgets have no declared form** (awkward). The spec puts budget optimization out of scope, but this is not optimization: spending buys a number of people reached, coverage is that number over the eligible (capped at 1), and each parameter moves from a baseline towards each program's outcome in proportion to its coverage. It is written here as three short expressions (`cov`, `outcome`, `switch`), with the budget and the switch as parameters set by each scenario. `ss.modify(..., set=...)` could not be used: its targets are named parameters or whole transitions, and the parameters here are expressions of counts (`diag`), used inside transitions. Suggested change: let `ss.modify` take an expression of counts and parameters as its amount, and let it target a named expression (`ss.par(expr)`); and, if programs recur, a `coverage` expression on a delivery (`ss.screen(test, capacity=budget/cost, eligible=sus | undx).coverage`) that a modifier can use.
5. **Fixed: `ss.by` was not an expression** (awkward). `1/ss.by(sex=[0.7, 1.2])` and `ss.by(...) - ss.by(...)` raised `TypeError`. **Fixed**: `ss.by` has arithmetic. But see finding 9 for what it does with an expression or another `ss.by`.
6. **Fixed: sums of states were not kept by group** (awkward). `res.plhiv.select(sex='females')` raised "not stratified". **Fixed**: a declared sum of states is a stratified result, and `compare.py` now compares the cascade (`plhiv`, `diagnosed`, `treated`) for each sex.
7. **Gone: branch shares were worked out before there was anyone** (minor). `p=[r/total ...]` in `leave()` was evaluated in `init_post()` before the initial counts were set, so NumPy printed "invalid value encountered in divide" twice. With `leave()` gone there are no branches, and no warning.
8. **Building the sim is most of the time** (speed). Of the 0.18 s, most is building, much of it `sc.search()` walking the expression trees in `init_dists()` and `try_functions()` (each program-affected rate is a switch of two expressions over seven coverages). Suggested change: skip expressions (which hold no distributions) when looking for distributions, and finding 2.
9. **New: arithmetic on `ss.by` swallows what it is combined with** (a bug; worked around). Since finding 5 was fixed, an `ss.by` on the left of an expression applies the operation to each of its values, so `ss.by(sex=[1, 2]) + I.n` gives `by(sex=[1 + I.n, 2 + I.n])`, which fails at run time with `TypeError: float() argument must be ... not 'BinOp'` (with `I.n + ss.by(...)` the result is an expression, and runs). In the same way, `ss.by(sex=[0.5, 0.6]) - ss.by(sex=[1.5, 2.2])` gives `by(sex=[by(sex=[-1.0, -1.7]), by(sex=[-0.9, -1.6])])`, an `ss.by` of `ss.by`s, not `by(sex=[-1.0, -1.6])`. The workarounds: the databook's values that are divided by counts are wrapped in `ss.par`, the expression is written first in `outcome`, and the differences are worked out with `np.subtract`. Suggested change: `by.__add__` and the like return `NotImplemented` for an expression (so the expression's own operator makes a `BinOp`), and combine two `ss.by`s over the same dimension level by level.
