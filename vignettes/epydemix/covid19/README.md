# epydemix COVID-19 in Massachusetts, in Starsim

The translation of [examples/epydemix/covid19](../../../examples/epydemix/covid19), epydemix's wave 3 example. The model is `model.py`; `compare.py` checks it against the original's reference results (it takes under two minutes).

## What was declared and what was not

Everything in the model is declared: six states, four transitions (an Erlang delay with `stages=4` in place of the original's four compartments, and a split on leaving `I` with `p=ss.by(age=IFR)` in place of two competing rates), a stratification by age with a contact matrix, a time series and a seasonal factor in the transmission rate, the weekly deaths as data, four parameters with bounds (one inside the seasonal factor and one in the initial conditions), and the reopening scenarios as `ss.modify` from 1 June. The weekly totals line up with the data without being asked: a flow is summed over the week ending at each data date. Since a date is the time point at the start of that day, this is the week to the end of the Saturday, a day earlier than epydemix's weeks ending on Sunday (`compare.py` sums its own weeks to the end of each Sunday).

The mobility data is turned into the contact reduction with pandas, as in the original. The transmission rate's scaling by R0 and the leading eigenvalue of the contact matrix is written out (`R0*mu/max(np.linalg.eigvals(matrix).real)`), as in the original.

Not translated: the random split of the initial infections between age groups (Starsim splits them in proportion to the population, the mean of the original's draw), and the posterior and projections from it (see the findings).

## Findings

Findings 1 to 4 and 6 were fixed in Starsim after the first version of this vignette; each says what fixed it.

1. **The chain-binomial solver substeps the whole model for one Erlang delay (blocked agreement; fixed by documenting it).** With a dwell time in four stages, `solver='binomial'` takes four substeps a day, for every transition, not just the staged one. epydemix takes one step a day, in which `1 - exp(-0.4)` = 33% of the infectious leave, so the infectious period is effectively 3 days, not 2.5. With substeps, deaths by 2 August are 6,039 instead of 10,525; `substeps=1` restores agreement to under 1%. The original is a discrete-time model in its own right, not an approximation of an ODE. *Fixed:* the solver now says in a note (`W102`) when it takes substeps for a staged dwell time, and that `substeps=1` takes whole steps; `substeps` is documented among the sim parameters. The vignette keeps `substeps=1`. Substepping only the staged transition would still be a better default for models like this one.
2. **A parameter used by two modules belonged to the first, silently (blocked calibration; fixed).** The first version built one disease per scenario with a function, and the parameters were registered only in the first, so `calibrate()` on any other said `E1301 NothingToFit`. *Fixed:* a parameter shared by several modules, or by a model built more than once, is now registered with each and fitted once. The scenarios stay as `ss.modify()`, which says what the original means (contacts 1.3 or 1.5 times higher from 1 June).
3. **An initial count could not be a parameter (fixed).** `init=dict(E=I0/2, I=I0/2)` raised a `TypeError`, and `init=dict(I=ss.par(...))` was not registered. *Fixed:* expressions of parameters in `init=` are evaluated and registered, and `I0` is now fitted, as in the original.
4. **A parameter inside `ss.seasonal` could not be fitted (fixed).** `ss.seasonal(mean=(1 + smin)/2, amplitude=(1 - smin)/(1 + smin))` with `smin` a parameter raised a `TypeError`. *Fixed:* expressions of parameters in `ss.seasonal` and `ss.timeseries` are evaluated at each step and registered, and `seasonality_min` is now fitted. A form given by the minimum (`ss.seasonal(low=0.8, ...)`) would still say what the original means more directly.
5. **No posterior (still open).** The original's calibration gives a posterior, and its projections are drawn from it; `sim.calibrate()` on a stochastic sim gives an Optuna point estimate. With four parameters fitted, the point lies inside the posterior on every parameter but gives quite different projections (see the table), which is exactly what a posterior is for. ABC-SMC is in the spec (`13-inference.md`) but not yet implemented. The original's distance (`wmape`) is Starsim's default goodness of fit. With the defaults (200 trials of one run) the search did not move from the starting values, which are the original's posterior medians; 1,000 trials of the mean of 5 runs did.
6. **Plotting failed with dated data (a bug; fixed).** `sim.plot()` and `fit.plot()` failed when the data's dates were strings, and `ss.parallel(...).plot()` failed even with parsed dates. *Fixed:* all three now plot, so the data is read with `ss.data()` and `model.py` ends with `msim.plot()`.
7. **`ss.data()` does not take a path object (a bug, new).** `ss.data(sc.thispath(__file__)/'deaths.csv')` fails with `ValueError: DataFrame constructor not properly called!` (in `data.load`, which reads a file only when the source is a `str`), so `model.py` passes `str(...)`. Suggested change: accept any `os.PathLike`.
8. **Many runs with a modifier leave the kernel (still open).** `sim.run(n_runs=)` is one kernel call for the persistence scenario, but with `ss.modify` it falls back to a `MultiSim`, which by default runs in parallel processes; `model.py` passes `parallel=False`, and `compare.py` runs them one by one. Already listed in `implementation.md`.
9. **Awkward: outcomes are not kept by group over many runs (still open).** Naming the transition `deaths` does the job without an `ss.outcome`. Already a known issue.
10. **No bundled population (still open).** As in the wave 1 example, the population of Massachusetts and its contact matrices are read from what epydemix downloaded; that is 6 of the 37 lines.
